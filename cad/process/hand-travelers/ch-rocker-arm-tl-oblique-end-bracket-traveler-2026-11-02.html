Not what the drawing says: thread depth (your ruling, 2026-11-02)

The drawing calls every hole 8-32 UNC THRU ALL or 6-32 UNC THRU ALL, threaded all the way through the 1 in height. A hand tap can't do that: it has only about 11/16 to 3/4 in of thread, and the shank behind the thread is bigger than the hole.

  • The tap drills go all the way through, as drawn.
  • The 4 #8-32 holes are drilled and tapped from the BAR SIDE (the face that sits on TL-20), because the base-bar screws come up from underneath.
  • The 2 #6-32 holes are drilled and tapped from the KP08 SIDE (the top face), because the KP08 screws go in from above.
  • Tap each hole until the end of the tap's thread reaches the surface, about 5/8 in. That leaves at least .45 in of full thread. Each screw needs about .35–.40 in. The last part of each hole stays at tap-drill size.

BEFORE YOU START

  • Safety glasses on. No gloves, rings or loose sleeves near the spindle. Clear chips with a brush, never your hand, and only with the spindle stopped.
  • Name the faces as you go, and keep them straight. INNER END: every X number is measured from it, and it's the first end you mill. ROW EDGE: one 1 in side face, which the hole row is measured .250 from. BAR SIDE and KP08 SIDE: the two 1/2 in faces. The holes are not centred along the length: the first hole is .285 from the inner end, and the last is .245 from the other end.
  • Small taps break. Turn by hand, one turn in and half a turn back. Take the tap right out every 3 or 4 turns to clear chips, and use plenty of tapping fluid. If it gets stiff, stop and back out. Never force it.
  • Drill only over open air. In Setups B and C the bar stands on edge across two parallels, with only a 1/4 in gap between them. The holes are in the middle of that gap with about 1/16 in to spare on each side. Push both parallels hard against the jaws, and look down the gap from the end before every hole.
  • Do each setup on both pieces before moving on to the next. Find X zero again for each piece.
  • Lessons from 10-30, built into this sheet: measure before you cut and write the allowance down. Cut to the DRO numbers on this sheet, and don't count passes. Conventional milling only: on the left end feed toward you, on the right end feed away from you.
  • Escape drill: if it rings or grabs, hit stop, wait for all motion to stop, then raise Z. Never back off sideways with the cutter in the cut.
  • Collet grip: wipe the oil off the collet and the shank, tighten the drawbar properly, and draw a marker line on the shank at the collet face. If the line moves, stop.

Op 10: Cut two blanks (saw)

  1. From the 1165 bar, after the 11.90 lever blank: cut two pieces 2.85 long. Scribe the line on Dykem and saw on the waste side.
  2. File off the burrs. Measure each blank and write it here: piece 1 ________ , piece 2 ________ . Each must be at least 2.75. The ends take off (that − 2.71) in total.

Setup A: Mill vise, bar flat. Square both ends to 2.71.

Looking down at the vise from where you stand. X is measured to the right from the inner end.

A1. Load

  1. Wipe the vise floor, the jaws and both 1 in parallels. Put one parallel against each jaw.
  2. Lay the piece flat on the parallels: a 1 in face up, with the jaws gripping across the 1 in width. About .2 in sticks out past the left side of the jaws.
  3. Tighten, tap down with the dead-blow hammer, tighten again. The parallels must not slide.

A2. Check the DRO

  1. Inch mode. Spindle moving right: X counts up. Spindle moving toward you: Y counts up.

A3. Mill the left end clean (1/2 in end mill, 1250 rpm). This is the INNER END.

  1. Spindle off: lower the cutter to about .04 below the bottom of the piece, beside the left end. Lock the head.
  2. Move in .02 in X, then feed toward you across the end. Repeat until the whole face is clean. Take no more than half of the allowance you wrote in Op 10.
  3. Write INNER END on the top face next to it, in marker.

A4. Move the piece

  1. Loosen, slide the piece right until about .2 in sticks out past the right side of the jaws, and tighten again. The INNER END is now inside the jaws, with an open gap between the jaws to its left.

A5. Find X on the inner end (edge finder, 1000 rpm)

  1. Lower the edge finder into the gap between the jaws just left of the inner end, with the tip about .1 below the top of the piece. Touch off the inner end from the left. Set X = −.100.

A6. Cut the right end to length (1/2 in end mill, 1250 rpm)

  1. Measure the end mill across its cutting edges and call it D (normally .500). Last pass at X = 2.710 + D/2: X 2.960 for D = .500. Yours: ________ .
  2. Spindle off: lower the cutter to about .04 below the bottom of the piece, beside the right end. Lock the head.
  3. .02 in passes, feeding away from you. Work in toward your last-pass X, and finish with a light pass exactly on it.
  4. Check: touch the edge finder on the right end. It should read 2.810; anything from 2.800 to 2.820 is OK. Check with calipers too: 2.700 – 2.720.

A7. Mark the faces (both pieces)

  1. Pick one 1 in side face and write ROW EDGE on it.
  2. Write BAR SIDE on one 1/2 in face and KP08 SIDE on the other.
  3. Measure the 1/2 in thickness (ROW EDGE to the opposite face) in the middle of the piece and write it here: piece 1 W = ________ , piece 2 W = ________ . You need it in Setup C.

Setup B: Bar on edge, BAR SIDE up. The four 8-32 holes.

B1. Load

  1. Two 1 in parallels, each pushed hard against a jaw. Stand the piece on edge on them: BAR SIDE up, ROW EDGE against the fixed (back) jaw, INNER END on the left, sticking out about .2 in past the left side of the jaws.
  2. Tighten, tap down, tighten again. About .23 in of the piece stands above the jaws.

B2. Set zero (edge finder, 1000 rpm)

  1. X: touch off the inner end from the left. Set X = −.100.
  2. Y: the ROW EDGE is against the fixed jaw, so use the jaw face. Raise the edge finder, move right to about X 3.5, past the end of the piece, and lower the tip into the gap between the jaws. Touch the fixed jaw from your side. Set Y = +.100. Y 0 is now the ROW EDGE.

  1. B3, B4: stop exactly on each number. Brush on oil, peck every .1, and stop as soon as the drill is through.
  2. B5, tapping (spindle power OFF at the switch): wrap a flag of masking tape round the 8-32 tap 5/8 in from its tip. Put the tap in the drill chuck, go to each hole, and add tapping fluid. Keep light pressure on the quill handle and turn the chuck by hand about 3 turns so the thread starts straight. Then finish with the T-handle wrench: one turn in, half a turn back, and take it right out every 3–4 turns to clear chips. Stop when the tape flag reaches the surface. Don't push the plain shank into the hole.

Setup C: Bar turned over, KP08 SIDE up. The two 6-32 holes.

C1. Load

  1. Roll the piece over along its length so the KP08 SIDE is up and the INNER END is still on the left. The ROW EDGE is now against the moving jaw, toward you. Parallels against the jaws, tighten, tap down, tighten again.

C2. Set zero (edge finder, 1000 rpm)

  1. X: touch off the inner end from the left. Set X = −.100.
  2. Y: touch the fixed jaw at about X 3.5, as in B2. Set Y = +.100. Y 0 is now the face opposite the ROW EDGE.
  3. Your row Y = W − .250, with W from A7. For W = .500 that's .250. Piece 1: ________ , piece 2: ________ .

Op 50: Deburr (bench)

  1. Run the deburring tool lightly round every edge, and round both ends of every hole: .010 max. Don't countersink the threads.
  2. Blow out or brush out the chips, and run each screw in once to clear the thread.
