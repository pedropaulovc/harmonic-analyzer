BEFORE YOU START

  • Safety glasses on. No gloves, rings or loose sleeves near the spindle. Aluminium chips are razor sharp: clear them with a brush, never your hand, and only with the spindle stopped.
  • Check your rail first. The seven threaded holes must line up with the holes in your HGR15R rail. Measure the rail: it should be 440 long, with 7 holes 60 apart and the first and last hole 40 from each end. If it isn't, stop and ask: the bar would be drilled to the wrong pattern.
  • The face you see on top in the vise becomes the bottom of the finished bar. The counterbores go in that face. This is on purpose, so the whole part is made in one setup. The picture under "Setup A" shows exactly what you should see.
  • Make sure the vise is lined up with the table. Run a dial indicator along the fixed jaw while you move the X axis. It should move less than 0.02 mm over the jaw length. If it doesn't, square the vise before you go on.
  • Drill only over open air. The parallels sit against the jaws. Every hole is placed clear of them, but look before every hole anyway. Never drill into a parallel or the vise.
  • The seven rail holes are the only tight feature (±0.08 mm). Stop exactly on the DRO number for every one of them. Everything else has plenty of tolerance.
  • The bar hangs about 140 mm out of the vise at each end. Don't lean on the ends, and don't hang anything on them while you cut.
  • If something squeals, chatters or grabs, stop the spindle first, then work out why.

Op 10: Cut the blanks (bandsaw)

  1. Cut two pieces 443 mm long from the 1/2 × 6 bar. That leaves 1.5 mm to clean up at each end.
  2. The bar is 152.4 wide. If your saw vise doesn't open past 152.4 mm, don't force it: clamp the bar flat on the saw bed with a C-clamp, or have the supplier cut it.
  3. File off the sharp saw burrs so the bar sits flat. Measure the width with calipers. It must be under 155 mm to fit the 6 in mill vise, which opens to 155.8 mm.

Op 20: Mark the bar

  1. Pick one long edge to be the ZERO EDGE, whichever looks straighter and cleaner. Write "ZERO EDGE" on the face next to it.
  2. Pick one end to be the ZERO END and write "ZERO END" there.
  3. On the same face, write "C'BORE SIDE — UP IN VISE". This face goes up in the mill.

Setup A: Mill vise. Everything is cut here, in one setup.

This is what you should see from where you stand, looking down at the vise. The zero corner is back-left. X is measured to the right from the zero end. Y is measured toward you from the zero edge. Every number on this sheet is a DRO reading from that corner.

Why it looks like a mirror image of the drawing: the drawing shows the other face. Through holes look the same from either side, and the counterbores really are on the face you are looking at ("C'BORE FAR SIDE" on the drawing).

Setup A operations (do them in this order for each bar)

A1. Load the bar

  1. Wipe the vise floor, the jaws and both 1 in parallels. Put one parallel against each jaw.
  2. Lay the bar on the parallels, marked face up, ZERO EDGE against the fixed (back) jaw, ZERO END on your left.
  3. Slide it sideways until roughly the same amount sticks out each side, about 140 mm. Measure with the rule.
  4. Tighten the vise firmly. Tap the bar down over the parallels with the dead-blow hammer, then tighten again. Try to slide each parallel with your fingers. If one moves, tap again until it doesn't.

A2. Check which way your DRO counts

  1. Move the table so the spindle goes to the right over the bar. X must count up.
  2. Move the table so the spindle comes toward you. Y must count up.
  3. If either counts down, reverse that axis in the DRO's setup menu (or ask someone who knows the readout). Every number below assumes both count up.

A3. Clean up the zero end (1/2 in end mill, 1250 rpm)

  1. Put the 1/2 in end mill in the collet. Spindle off: lower the head until the end of the cutter sits about 1 mm below the bottom of the bar, beside the left end. There is nothing under that end, so this is safe. Lock the head.
  2. Bring the cutter to the left of the left end, toward the back. Start the spindle and brush on oil.
  3. Move in 0.5 mm in X, then feed toward you (Y handwheel) right across the end, about one turn every 2 seconds. Come back out past the end, move in another 0.5 mm, and repeat.
  4. Stop once the whole end face is shiny and clean, with no saw marks left. Don't take off more than 2.5 mm in total, or the bar will end up too short.

A4. Set zero (edge finder, 1000 rpm)

  1. Put the edge finder in the 3/8 collet. At 1000 rpm, push its tip slightly off-centre so it wobbles.
  2. X zero: at about the middle of the end (Y around 75), creep toward the left end face from the left. When the tip suddenly kicks sideways, stop. Set X = −2.54, half the tip's diameter, because the spindle centre is still that far outside the bar.
  3. Y zero: raise the finder, move right to about X 100 so you are past the vise jaw, and lower it behind the bar. Creep toward the back edge from behind until it kicks. Set Y = −2.54.
  4. Check it: raise the finder and go to X 0.000, Y 0.000. The finder tip should sit right over the back-left corner.
  5. If your DRO has an edge-finder function, it can do the 2.54 offset for you (tip diameter 5.08).

A5. Cut the bar to length (1/2 in end mill, 1250 rpm)

  1. Measure the end mill across its cutting edges with calipers. Call that D (normally 12.70).
  2. The cutter centre must finish at X = 440.00 + D/2. With D = 12.70 that is X 446.35.
  3. Lower the cutter about 1 mm below the bar bottom, beside the right end, as in A3. Start well to the right, then take 0.5 mm cuts, feeding away from you (toward the column) across the end each time. The last pass is at X 446.35.
  4. The bar is now 440.0 long (anything from 439.2 to 440.8 is OK).

A6 to A9. Spot and drill every hole (drill chuck)

For each hole: move to the X and Y in the table below, stop exactly on the number, then feed the quill down. Brush on oil. Peck: drill about 3 mm, pull out to clear the chips, and repeat. When the drill breaks through you'll feel it drop. Don't go more than about 3 mm past the bottom.

A10. Tap the 7 rail holes 8-32 (by hand, spindle power OFF)

  1. Switch the mill off at the switch. The spindle must not be able to start.
  2. Put the 8-32 tap in the drill chuck. Go to R1 (X 40.00, Y 88.90). Put a drop of tapping fluid in the hole.
  3. Lower the quill until the tap sits in the hole. Keep light downward pressure on the quill handle and turn the chuck clockwise by hand about 3 turns. This starts the thread dead straight.
  4. Loosen the chuck, lift the quill off the tap, and finish with the T-handle tap wrench: one turn in, half a turn back to break the chip, all the way through. If it suddenly gets stiff, stop, back out a full turn, add fluid, and carry on. An 8-32 tap snaps easily, and a broken tap is very hard to get out.
  5. Back the tap out, clear the chips with a brush, and repeat for R2 to R7.

A11. Counterbore the 8 bracket holes (5/16 in end mill, 2000 rpm)

  1. Put the 5/16 in end mill in its collet. Go to B1. With the spindle running, lower it until it just grabs a cigarette paper laid on the bar. That is the top of the bar. Zero the quill depth readout there (or note the reading).
  2. Set the quill stop so the cutter can go 4.80 mm deep (4.29 to 5.31 is OK).
  3. At each bracket hole B1 to B8, plunge slowly to the stop with oil, then come straight back up.
  4. After B1, drop a #8 socket screw into the hole. Its head must sit below the bar surface: lay a steel rule across it, and the rule must not rock.

A12. Mill the 2 slots (1/2 in end mill, 1250 rpm)

Each slot is 13.50 wide, with its two round ends 6.00 apart. The bar is held to the table later by a 1/2 in stud through each slot.

Go down another 3 mm and repeat. Stop when the cutter end is about 0.5 mm below the bar bottom, which is 5 passes: 3, 6, 9, 12 and 13.2 mm.

Go clockwise as seen from above: right along the back side, toward you down the right side, left along your side, away from you up the left side.
  3. If your end mill isn't 12.70, move the lap's X values out by (13.50 − D) ÷ 2 each side of the centre X, and the Y values by the same amount beyond the end centres.
  4. Measure the slot width with calipers: 12.99 to 14.01. If it's under 12.99, move the lap out 0.1 more each side and go round again.

A13. Unload

Before you loosen the vise, check the length on the DRO: touch the edge finder on the right end. It should read 442.54, which is 440.0 + 2.54 (OK from 441.74 to 443.34). Then brush off the chips and take the bar out.

Op 30: Deburr (bench)

  1. Run the deburring tool lightly around every edge: all four long edges on both faces, both ends, and both ends of every hole and slot.
  2. Light touch: one or two passes. The drawing allows a break of 0.25 mm at most, about the thickness of three sheets of paper. Don't make big chamfers.
  3. Run an 8-32 screw through each rail hole to clear any burr pushed into the thread.
