# M11 — Soldering, finishing and assembly craft

## Objectives

- Make a soldered joint you can rely on
- Finish a part to the standard the book will photograph
- Assemble precision parts without damaging them

## Prerequisites

- m03
- m10 — the "now make" is soldering the cone gear set to its shaft, so the
  gears have to exist first. (The soft/silver-solder *practice* can be done any
  time after m03; only the real joint waits on m10.)

## References

- `references/machining-for-hobbyists-getting-started/`
- `docs/machining-dfm.md` (gears soldered, no keys)
- `cad/config/parts/*.yaml` (appearance per part)

## Practice

- Soft vs silver solder: strength, temperature, and when each is right
- Cleanliness, flux, and heating brass next to steel
- Fixturing a joint that must stay square and concentric
- Draw filing, stoning, polishing brass, blacking steel
- Matching the original: matte-black rods, bright amplitude bars, black-stained crank handle

## Now make — the real part this unlocks

The **cone gear set soldered to its shaft** — twenty gears, in position, on one shaft, concentric. Then finish the `crank-handle` and a `connecting-rod`.

## Competency check

A test joint pulled to destruction that fails in the parent metal, not the joint; and a polished brass surface with no visible scratch pattern.

## Notes

There is not a single keyway in this machine. The soldered cone-gear joints are structural and must be concentric — get this right on scrap first.
