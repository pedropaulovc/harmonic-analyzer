# M02 — Lathe I — facing, turning, shoulders

## Objectives

- Face a bar square
- Turn to a diameter and hit it
- Cut a shoulder to a length
- Get a finish you would put in a book

## Prerequisites

- m01

## References

- `references/machining-for-hobbyists-getting-started/07_chapter-5-introduction-to-lathes.pdf`
- `references/machining-for-hobbyists-getting-started/08_chapter-6-working-on-lathe.pdf`
- `references/machinerys-handbook/` (speeds and feeds)

## Practice

- Tool grinding or insert selection; centre height
- Roughing vs finishing: why the last 0.05 mm behaves differently
- Speeds and feeds for brass and mild steel at 5-25 mm diameters
- Chatter: recognising it and the four things that cause it
- Taper: diagnosing headstock alignment vs tool deflection

## Now make — the real part this unlocks

**`crank-pin`** and a **`pivot-shaft`** blank — plain turned steel, no bore, no step. The first real parts of the analyzer.

## Competency check

Five consecutive parts turned to a nominal diameter within ±0.02 mm, with a finish that needs no filing.

## Notes

Brass and steel behave completely differently. Do both from the start — this machine is mostly brass, and brass punishes a tool with positive rake.
