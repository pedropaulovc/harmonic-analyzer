No — the difference between a keyed and a keyless mill drill chuck is not just convenience. Here’s a breakdown:

### Keyed Chuck
- **Mechanism**: Uses a toothed gear and pinion system tightened with a chuck key.
- **Pros**:
  - **Stronger grip** on the tool shank — better for heavy cuts and larger drill bits.
  - Less likely to loosen under load or vibration.
- **Cons**:
  - Requires a chuck key (which can be lost).
  - Slower tool changes.

### Keyless Chuck
- **Mechanism**: Hand-tightened, often with dual-sleeve or single-sleeve design.
- **Pros**:
  - **Faster tool changes**, no key needed.
  - Often sufficient for light to medium-duty work.
- **Cons**:
  - May slip under heavy loads or vibration, especially in larger sizes or on harder materials.
  - Grip strength depends on hand torque and chuck design (higher-end models mitigate this).

### Recommendation for PM-728VT
For your mill:
- Use a **keyed chuck** (like a Jacobs 14N or Albrecht with a key) for **larger drills**, **tougher materials** (e.g., steel), or **precision work**.
- A **keyless chuck** is fine for light-duty use or rapid tooling in softer materials (e.g., aluminum, plastic).
